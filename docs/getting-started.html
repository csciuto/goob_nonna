Getting Started

You don't need to know anything about synthesizers to start making sounds. This guide gives you enough to experiment — the best way to learn is to twist knobs and listen.

The Big Idea

A synthesizer generates a sound, shapes it, and controls when you hear it. Goob Nonna does this in a chain:

Oscillators → Mixer → Filter → Amplifier → Reverb → Speakers

Each link in the chain does one job. That's it. Everything else is just controlling how each link does its job.

The Modules

Oscillators: Where Sound Begins

Oscillators produce raw tones. You have two (OSC 1 and OSC 2), and each can produce four waveforms. The waveform you pick is your starting material — everything downstream sculpts it.

WaveformCharacterGood For
SawtoothBright, buzzy, rich in harmonicsBasses, leads, brass, strings — the workhorse
SquareHollow, woody, odd harmonics onlyClarinets, retro leads, sub basses
TriangleSoft, pure, almost sinusoidalFlutes, sub basses, gentle pads
Narrow PulseThin, nasal, reedyOboe-like tones, plucky sounds

Octave (the footage setting: 32', 16', 8', 4', 2') shifts pitch by whole octaves. 8' is "normal." Lower numbers = higher pitch.

Frequency detunes the oscillator by semitones. Set OSC 2 slightly off from OSC 1 (try ±0.1 to ±0.3) to get a thick, chorused sound. This is one of the simplest and most satisfying things you can do.

Mixer: Blending Sources

Three volume knobs — one per source (OSC 1, OSC 2, Noise). Turn them up to hear them, down to mute them. Noise is useful for adding breath, texture, or making percussion.

Filter: Sculpting the Tone

The filter removes frequencies from the sound. Think of it as a tone control with superpowers.

  • Cutoff — the most important knob on the synth. Sweep it and listen. It controls which frequencies pass through. Low = dark and muffled. High = bright and open.
  • Resonance — boosts frequencies right at the cutoff point. Low values add subtle emphasis. High values create a sharp, whistling peak. At maximum, the filter self-oscillates and becomes its own sound source.
  • ENV AMT — how much the envelope (see below) moves the cutoff. Positive values open the filter on each note, then close it. Negative values do the reverse. This is how you get that classic "wah" on each keypress.
  • KB TRACK — makes the filter cutoff follow the keyboard. At 1:1, higher notes sound brighter (like a real instrument). At 0, every note has the same brightness regardless of pitch.

Envelope: Shaping Notes Over Time

The envelope controls how a note evolves from the moment you press a key to after you release it. Four sliders:

/\ / \___________ / \ \ / \ \ A D S R
  • Attack — how fast the sound reaches full volume (0 = instant snap, high = slow swell)
  • Decay — how fast it drops from the peak to the sustain level
  • Sustain — the level held while you keep the key pressed (this is a level, not a time)
  • Release — how long the sound fades after you let go

Quick recipes:

  • Pluck/percussion: fast A, fast D, zero S, short R
  • Pad/strings: slow A, medium D, high S, long R
  • Organ: zero A, zero D, full S, zero R

The envelope also modulates the filter (via ENV AMT), which is where most of the expressive character comes from.

VCA: The Volume Gate

The VCA controls whether you hear anything. Three modes:

  • ENV — the envelope controls volume. This is what you want 95% of the time.
  • KB RLS — instant attack, but uses the envelope's release time when you let go. Good for organ-like sounds with a fade-out.
  • DRONE — always on. Sound plays continuously. Useful with the sequencer or for ambient textures.

LFO: Automatic Movement

The LFO (Low Frequency Oscillator) is too slow to hear as a tone — instead it wobbles other things. The Mod Wheel controls how much it affects:

  • PITCH AMT — vibrato (wobbling pitch). A little goes a long way.
  • CUTOFF AMT — wah-wah effect (wobbling the filter). Classic.
  • PW AMT — pulse width modulation (wobbling the shape of pulse waves). Gives a swirling, animated quality.

Try different LFO waveforms: Triangle for smooth wobble, Square for trills, S/H (Sample & Hold) for random robotic steps.

Reverb

One knob: MIX. Turn it up for space, down for dry. Simple.

Interesting Default Interactions

These things happen automatically without patching any cables:

  1. Envelope → Filter + VCA — Every keypress opens the filter and controls volume simultaneously. This is why the synth sounds musical out of the box. Crank ENV AMT to hear the filter sweep.
  2. Two detuned oscillators — Turn up OSC 2's level, set both to sawtooth, and detune OSC 2 slightly. Instant thick analog sound. This is the foundation of countless classic synth patches.
  3. Resonance + Envelope — High resonance + moderate ENV AMT creates an aggressive, squelchy attack on each note. The "acid" sound.
  4. LFO + Mod Wheel — The mod wheel scales all three LFO destinations at once. So you can set up pitch vibrato, filter wobble, and PWM, then bring them all in gradually with the wheel.
  5. Keyboard Tracking + Filter — With KB TRACK at 1:1, the filter opens as you play higher notes. Without it, high notes sound dull. This is subtle but crucial for playable sounds.

Patching: The Modular Part

The jacks on the panel let you rewire the synth. There are three kinds:

  • Outputs (red-tinted rings) — sources of signal
  • Inputs (blue-tinted rings) — destinations that accept signal
  • Mult (gray rings) — four jacks wired together; each works as either

To patch: Click an output jack, then click an input jack. A cable appears. Click the cable to remove it, or right-click a jack to unplug its cable. Each jack can only hold one cable.

The key concept: Audio inputs (the mixer's three inputs, the filter's INPUT, VCA IN, REVERB IN) and the envelope's TRIGGER IN replace whatever was connected there by default. Pull the cable out, and the default connection comes back. Control inputs (PITCH IN, CUTOFF IN, RATE IN and the rest) add to what the knobs and keyboard are already doing. Either way you can't break anything permanently — just unplug.

Pitch inputs follow the one-volt-per-octave standard, and the LFO and envelope are strong signals: straight into PITCH IN or CUTOFF IN they sweep several octaves. Run them through the Attenuator first for something subtler.

Patch Ideas to Try

S/H OUT → CUTOFF IN — Random filter cutoff on every LFO cycle. Instant robot.
+ENV OUT → ATTENUATOR → OSC 1 PITCH IN — Envelope controls pitch. Notes swoop up or down on each keypress; the attenuator sets how far and which way.
WAVE OUT → ATTENUATOR → OSC 2 PITCH IN — Vibrato on one oscillator only, independent of the mod wheel.
KB VEL OUT → CUTOFF IN — Velocity-sensitive filter with a MIDI keyboard (harder playing = brighter).
WAVE OUT (square) → TRIGGER IN — The LFO fires the envelope by itself: a rhythm with no keys held.
GATE OUT → SYNC IN, RATE at minimum, S/H OUT → CUTOFF IN — A new random filter setting with every key.
KB OUT → RATE IN — The LFO tracks the keyboard, fast enough to be a third oscillator.
OSC 1 WAVE OUT → OSC 2 LIN FM IN — Metallic, bell-like tones.
RESONANCE all the way up, mixer knobs down, KBD TRACK 1:1 — No cables: the filter sings a sine wave you can play.

The Utility Modules

  • Mult — Splits one signal into copies. Patch something into any one jack, take it from the others.
  • Attenuator — Scales a signal down, or inverts it (center is off). With nothing plugged into its input it puts out a steady voltage you set with the knob: patch its OUTPUT into PITCH IN to transpose, or into RATE IN to push the LFO past its knob.
  • High Pass — Removes low frequencies, gently. Patch audio through it to thin things out.

The Rear Panel

Under the ⋮ menu: CLK IN steps the arpeggiator or sequencer from any pulse (try the LFO's square wave), ON/OFF IN starts and stops it, RESET IN sends it back to the first step, and CLK OUT pulses on every step.

Arpeggiator & Sequencer

The ARP/SEQ module and the three colored buttons below the GLIDE knob work together.

Using the Arpeggiator

  1. Make sure the MODE switch is set to ARP
  2. Press the PLAY button (green) to activate the arpeggiator
  3. Hold down some notes on the keyboard — the arp will play them one at a time rhythmically
  4. Use the RATE knob to change speed (20–280 BPM)
  5. Try different DIRECTION settings: ORDR plays notes as you played them, FWD/BKWD ping-pongs, RNDM shuffles
  6. Set OCT/SEQ to 2 or 3 to spread the pattern across octaves
  7. Press HOLD (blue) to latch — you can lift your fingers and the pattern keeps going. Play new notes to add them. Lift all fingers first if you want to start a new pattern.
  8. Press PLAY again to stop

Recording a Sequence

  1. Set MODE to REC and OCT/SEQ to 1, 2, or 3 (selects which slot to record into)
  2. Play notes on the keyboard — each note becomes a step. The first note erases any existing sequence in that slot.
  3. Use the buttons for special steps:
    • PLAY → TIE: press TIE then play a note to tie it to the previous step (legato)
    • HOLD → REST: inserts a silent step
    • TAP → ACCENT: press before a note to accent it
  4. When done, switch MODE to SEQ

Playing Back a Sequence

  1. Set MODE to SEQ and OCT/SEQ to the slot you recorded
  2. Press PLAY (green)
  3. Press a note on the keyboard to set the starting pitch (transposes the sequence)
  4. Use RATE to adjust speed
  5. Press PLAY again to stop

Tap Tempo

Tap the TAP button (yellow) at least three times at your desired tempo. This overrides the RATE knob. To return control to the knob, hold TAP for about one second until its light turns off.

MIDI Keyboard

Press MIDI IN (next to the presets) and allow MIDI when the browser asks. A connected keyboard now plays the synth, through the arpeggiator and sequencer too. Its pitch and mod wheels move the ones on screen, and how hard you play comes out of KB VEL OUT: patch that somewhere to hear it. If the keyboard or your DAW sends MIDI clock, the arpeggiator follows it and the RATE knob chooses the note value. Press MIDI IN again to turn it off. Works in Chrome, Edge and Firefox.

Saving and Sharing a Patch

The address bar always holds your patch: every knob, switch and cable. Bookmark it to keep a sound, or copy it to send one. Loading a preset sets the whole panel, and Default clears it.

Presets start with the Mod Wheel half up. Most of them get their motion from the LFO, so move the wheel while you play: all the way down, many go still or quiet; all the way up, they get wilder.

Keyboard Octave Shift

Hold Shift on your computer keyboard, then click PLAY to shift down one octave or TAP to shift up. Both the on-screen keys and QWERTY keys change pitch together. A toast shows the new range.

Glide (Portamento)

The GLIDE knob (next to the buttons) adds a pitch slide between notes. Turn it up and play two notes in sequence — you'll hear the pitch smoothly sweep from one to the other. At maximum, the slide takes about 2 seconds.

Legato glide: Hold Shift and turn the GLIDE knob to the right (a toast says "LEGATO GLIDE ON"). Now the synth only slides between overlapping notes — hold one key, press another while still holding the first. The pitch slides without retriggering the envelope (no click). If you release the first key before pressing the next, there's no slide and the envelope starts fresh. Turn Shift+GLIDE left to turn legato off.

First Experiment

Tip: Hover over any knob, switch, slider, or patch jack to see a tooltip explaining what it does. Use the kebab menu (⋮) to switch keyboard labels between shortcuts, note names, or off.

  1. Play some notes — click the keyboard or use your computer keyboard (Z-M bottom row, Q-P top row)
  2. Sweep the CUTOFF knob slowly from high to low and back
  3. Turn up RESONANCE to about 2-3 and sweep cutoff again
  4. Set ENV AMT to about 3, attack to 0, decay to about 3, sustain to 0 — now each note has a filter sweep
  5. Turn up OSC 2 level, detune it slightly — notice the thickening
  6. Push the MOD WHEEL up with some CUTOFF AMT — hear the wobble
  7. Try a preset from the dropdown to hear what's possible

Tip: Double-click any knob or slider to reset it to its default value.

From here, just experiment. Every knob does something audible. You can't damage anything.
